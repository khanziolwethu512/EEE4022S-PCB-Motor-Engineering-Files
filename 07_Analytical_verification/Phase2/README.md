# EEE4022S AK-07: Analytical verification, Phase 2 (22 October 2026)

**Independent analytical electromagnetic model of the original Rev0 and Rev1-S designs, compared with the archived FEM.**

Geometry is the original design throughout:
- 86 mm OD / 19 mm bore / 2 mm steel backplates;
- 16 N35 magnets, 20 × 6 × 2 mm, on r = 28 mm;
- 1.0 mm magnet-to-PCB gap and 1.0 mm R1-steel-to-S2 clearance;
- five 1.02032 mm boards per stator.

The manufactured prototype geometry is **not** used.

## 0. How sources are kept apart

| Label | Meaning | Examples here |
|---|---|---|
| **ANALYTICAL** | Computed in this package from geometry, layout copper paths and magnet material data. No Maxwell output enters it | K, ψ, back-EMF and harmonics, B, layout R |
| **FEM** | Archived Ansys results, used **only for comparison**, plus clearly marked FEM inputs in the operating analysis | Field map, detailed-copper board models, full-motor Rev0, rotating model, inductance, Icepak R_th, eddy loss |
| **MEASURED** | Bench measurement already taken (resistance only). No new experiments; testing remains postponed | 1.87–1.90 Ω per stator phase |

`analytical_em.py` reads no FEM file. FEM values appear only in `compare.py` and as labelled inputs in `operating.py`.

## 1. Inputs and provenance

**Copper paths.** `traces_board.json` is generated by `make_traces_board.py` from the ODB++ manufacturing export (numpy/scipy/shapely, no Ansys).
- It contains every track centre-line (arcs as 0.4 mm chords) on L1–L6, plus every via step.
- A DC nodal solve of the copper network, 1 A from In (L1) to Out (L6), gives each piece's current share. This handles the parallel L2/L3 and L4/L5 copper and the dead-end stubs.
- Phase B and C patterns are the real B and C copper, not rotated copies.
- The board and layer z positions come from the stack-up.

**Magnets.**
- Uniform axial magnetisation, alternating N/S, with magnet 1 centred on +x and north towards +z.
- Br = 1.23 T and μr = 1.0998. This is the N35 material assigned in the original design, used as **material data**, not as a result.
- The N35 manufacturer range of 1.17–1.22 T is covered in the sensitivity table.

**Steel.** Treated as infinitely permeable planes with no saturation. The FEM steel peaks at 0.84–0.93 T, so it is far from saturation.

## 2. Method

1. **Magnet field.** Each magnet is two charged sheets, ±σ with σ = M on its faces. The field of a uniformly charged rectangle is closed-form (arcsinh/arctan), and `sheet_test.py` checks it against brute-force quadrature to 5 significant figures.
2. **Steel.** Modelled by images.
   - **Rev0, and Rev1-S S1:** one plane, the backplate face. A magnet on its plate becomes the doubled-thickness magnet. R2 and its magnets sit behind R1's plate and are screened.
   - **Rev1-S S2, explicitly different from S1:** S2 lies between two planes, R1's bare steel below and R2's backplate above. This gives an infinite image series, truncated at n = 4 (n = 2 and n = 6 change K by 0.01 %).
   - **Effect of the R1 steel below S2:** it raises the board next to it, board 5 of S2, from 2.005 to 2.857 mN·m/A. That is +42 % over the identical board position in S1.
3. **Recoil permeability.** M_eff = M / (1 + (μr − 1) N_z). Here N_z is the volume-average demagnetising factor of the image-equivalent magnet, computed with the same sheet formula: 0.536 for a magnet on one plate and 0.515 for R2 between two plates. This choice was fixed before any comparison was run.
4. **Lorentz torque.** Computed on every track piece and via step at its current share, T_z = [r × (I dl × B)]_z. Reciprocity then gives k(θ) = dλ/dθ = −T_copper/I.
   - The rotor is turned through one electrical period, 16 angles.
   - From k(θ): the fundamental K1, the phase ψ and the 3rd, 5th and 7th harmonics.
   - Stators and machine are summed in series.
5. **Back-EMF.** e = ω_m·k(θ), so E_ph,rms = K1·ω_m/√2. In the line voltage the triplen harmonics cancel.

## 4. What the comparison shows

1. **The magnetic field is independently confirmed.**
   - The analytical model and the FEM field map share no code and no field data. Both integrate the same layout copper paths.
   - They agree within ±1.2 % on every board, within 0.3 % on every stator and phase, and within 0.1° in phase.
   - This includes the different S2 environment: S2 is 13.6 % stronger than S1 analytically against 13.5 % in the field map. So the steel-image treatment of R1's bare plate is right, and the field map's field is right.
2. **The ~5 % gap to the detailed-copper FEM is not a field error.**
   - It is equally present in the field map, and it grows with distance from the magnets: +1.4 % at board 1, rising to +10 % at board 5. This is the trend first noted on 8 Oct.
   - Averaging over the track cross-section removes only 0.07 %, so the centre-line (filament) representation is **not** the cause.
   - What is left differs between the filament models (current shares from a DC network of 0.5 mm tracks) and the detailed FEM (current solved in the solid copper). Candidates:
     - current distribution in wide copper (pads, terminal islands, solid vias);
     - a field-resolution limit of the detailed model's adaptive mesh far from the magnets;
     - layer-position detail.
   - The gap is **not resolved by this phase**. Section 7 gives the targeted FEM test that separates these candidates.
3. **Independent bracket on K.** The detailed-copper FEM values, 18.53 and 39.50 mN·m/A, remain the reference values. The analytical model independently confirms them to +5.2 % (Rev0) and +5.5 % (Rev1-S), which brackets K between the two methods. It also reproduces the phase angle, the EMF harmonic content and the S2/S1 ratio.
4. **The recoil correction matters as much as the residual gap**, at about 5 %. With μr = 1 the analytical K would read +10.8 % against the detailed FEM. The N35 Br tolerance is of similar size (up to −4.9 %).

## 5. Layout resistance (analytical) against FEM and measured

| | Board-phase (Ω) | Stator phase, 5 boards (Ω) |
|---|---|---|
| **ANALYTICAL, layout** (ρ = 1.724e-8 Ω·m, 0.5 mm × 35.0 / 35.6 µm tracks, vias solid or 20 µm plated) | **0.2657 – 0.2697** | **1.329 – 1.348** |
| FEM DC conduction (solid vias) | 0.2774 – 0.2779 | 1.389 |
| MEASURED (2-wire, temperature not recorded) | 0.32 – 0.34 | 1.87 – 1.90 (with joints and leads) |

The layout value is 4 % below the FEM, which includes pads, terminal islands and current crowding at bends. The measured value is 20–28 % higher per board than the layout value. That points to thinner finished copper and/or narrower etched tracks than nominal, plus lead and contact resistance; the copper has not been sectioned to separate these. The operating analysis uses the **measured** resistance, because it is the real winding.

**Inductance.** Not computed analytically in this phase. The earlier Neumann estimate of about 110 µH was withdrawn as 2–3× too high. Until an analytical estimate agrees with the FEM, the FEM armature-only value (34–51 µH per stator) is used, labelled FEM. Inductance changes the operating results by under 1 %: X ≈ 0.04 Ω at 1000 rpm against R ≈ 2 Ω.

## 6. Consistent operating analysis (`operating.py`)

**Assumptions.**
- Speed, current and voltage are not independent; the assumptions fix them:
  - star connection;
  - FOC with i_d = 0;
  - SVPWM inverter with m = 0.95: 12 V dc for Rev0, 24 V dc for Rev1-S (series).
- Steady state: |V| = √((RI + E)² + (ω_e L I)²) ≤ V_max.
- Thermal limit: 60 °C board in 25 °C air, with copper loss at R(T) plus track eddy loss scaled as n².
  - The 25 °C air is the same basis as Test D and the rev. D operating points.
  - The eddy loss is FEM: 20 / 44 mW at 1000 rpm.
- Thermal resistance: FEM Icepak (8.75 and 14.8 K/W per stator) or the cautious basis (15 and 25.3 K/W). Both are conditional; neither is measured.

**Method.** At each speed, the largest current meeting the voltage and thermal limits is found. Each case runs twice, once with the **analytical K** and once with the **FEM K**.

**Results at 1000 rpm, thermally limited.**

| Rev0 | FEM | cautious | 0.840 | 23.3 | 4.63 | 51 % |

| Rev0 | FEM | Icepak | 1.101 | 30.6 | 5.33 | 44 % |
| Rev1-S | analytical | cautious | 0.644 | 40.2 | 8.79 | 60 % |

**Observations.**
- **Current limit.** The thermal current limit does not depend on K; it depends only on R and R_th. The K source therefore changes the torque and voltage, not the current.
- **Speed range.** Both machines are thermally limited up to about 2000–2400 rpm and voltage-limited above.
- **No-load speed:**
  - Rev0 at 12 V: 3224 rpm (analytical K) or 3391 rpm (FEM K).
  - Rev1-S at 24 V: 3018 or 3182 rpm.
  - These are electrical limits only; the mechanics are still limited to 1000 rpm.
- **Earlier points reproduced.** With the FEM K the analysis reproduces the rev. D operating points: 0.84 A pk and 23.3 mN·m for Rev0, 0.644 A pk and 38.2 mN·m for Rev1-S.

The full sweep is in `out/operating_envelope.csv`, with the envelope plotted in `O1_torque_speed_envelope.png`.

## 7. Next: targeted FEM verification (proposed, not yet run)

**Purpose.** Locate the 5 % filament-versus-detailed gap.

**Test.** Re-solve the detailed single-board model at the board-5 position (5.08 mm), and at board 1 as a control.
- In the **same** solved project, export B on that board's layout centre-lines and integrate the filament torque.
- Compare it with Maxwell's own copper (Lorentz) and rotor (virtual-work) torque.

**How to read the outcome.**
- **Filament torque on the detailed model's B equals the detailed torque:** the field in the detailed model is weaker at board 5 than in the field map and the analytical model. This points to a mesh or field-resolution limit in the detailed model. Follow-up: refine the mesh in the board region and check whether the detailed K rises.
- **Filament torque on the detailed model's B is about 10 % above the detailed torque:** the field agrees. The difference lies in where the current actually flows in the solid copper (pads, islands, vias) compared with the network shares.

**Cost.** About 2 solves × 4 angles on a lab PC or the HPC.

**After that.** The planned v11 batch already provides the FEM current sweeps, speed-dependent losses, as-built sensitivity and Rev2-S. Their results will be added to Sections 3 and 6 in the same three-way format.

## 8. Limitations and error sources (analytical model)

- **Steel:** infinite plane, infinite μ, unsaturated. Finite OD (43 mm radius against 38 mm magnet outer radius), the 19 mm bore and finite μ are neglected. The plates are well below saturation, so the error is small, but it is not quantified.
- **Magnets:** ideal uniform axial magnetisation. The recoil treatment is linear and volume-averaged, and the same N_z is used everywhere in a magnet. Magnet-to-magnet interaction through μr is neglected. Br is taken as nominal.
- **Conductors:** current shares come from a DC network of 0.5 mm tracks (filaments; cross-section averaging tested at −0.07 %). Pads and terminal copper are treated as the same tracks. There is no skin or proximity effect, which is valid at 133 Hz.
- **Excluded effects:** no armature reaction (FEM shows the winding field is 300–400 times weaker than the magnets'), no eddy currents, no end effects of the leads.
- **Numerics:** 16 rotor angles (32 angles changes K by 0.01 %) and image series truncated at n = 4 (0.01 %).

## Reproduce

```
python sheet_test.py           # closed-form sheet field vs quadrature
python analytical_em.py        # primary analytical model -> out/analytical_primary.json (numpy only)
python compare.py <fem_dir>    # all cases + FEM comparison -> out/comparison_tables.md, A1/A2 figures (about 20 min)
python resistance.py           # layout resistance
python operating.py            # consistent operating envelope -> out/operating_envelope.*, O1 figure
```

`<fem_dir>` holds `FIELDMAP_REV0_P20_results` and `FIELDMAP_REV1S_P20_results`, which are included under `fem_reference/` for comparison only.
